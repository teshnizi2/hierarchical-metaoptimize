The headline, stated against us

CORRECTIONS 171 · 175

MetaOptimize does not beat a tuned plain baseline.

Nobody in this corpus had run a tuned non-meta baseline outside CIFAR-10. On CIFAR-100 it now exists, and the family sits 5.699 pp below tuned SGD — 18.80 SE, measured in batch — with every rung of the SGD learning-rate ladder, including the worst, beating the best MetaOptimize cell the corpus owns. On CIFAR-10 the deficit is 1.46 pp and does not close with budget.

That reframes the contribution rather than ending it.

The paper this campaign can honestly write is not “MetaOptimize wins.” It is a partition audit: within the method, how the step-size parameters are grouped is worth 25–47 pp, the grouping that matters is a handful of named tensors rather than a count, and the whole effect survives tuning each arm at its own optimum.

1,536 parameters carry the gap

figure 2 · CORRECTIONS 185, 187

A probe on the scalar trajectory found three layer4 BatchNorm scales — layer4.0.bn2.weight, layer4.0.shortcut.1.weight, layer4.1.bn2.weight, 0.014 % of the model — carrying the Lion sign against the other 59 tensors on 944 of 944 pinned records. ciso1 tested that causally: move exactly those three into their own step-size group and nothing else.

traintest — layerwise ceilingtest — at the scalar floor

What this does not yet establish.

ISO and CTRL differ in tensor identity and network depth and isolated parameter count at once, so the design cannot say which is operative — ResNet-18 has exactly five 512-wide BatchNorm scales and all five are in layer4, so a depth-matched, size-matched, carrier-free control had to be built separately. That arm is running. The verdict stands at UNRESOLVED-PARTIAL until it lands.

The gap survives tuning

figure 3 · RULE 11 · CORRECTIONS 174

The obvious objection to any granularity result is that one arm was simply run further from its own optimum. Comparing each arm at its own best meta step size instead of a shared one shrinks the gap — but does not remove it. It retains 74.4 % of its size.

What the carriers have in common

figure 5 · CORRECTIONS 188.3, 189.2

ResNet-18 has exactly five 512-wide BatchNorm scales and all five sit in layer4. Three are the carriers. Reading them against BasicBlock.forward on the live model gives a sharp split — and reading them against the probe gives an equally sharp one, on a different axis.

carrier · output is a summand of a residual addnon-carrier · sits behind a ReLU, feeds no add

Two confounds, both stated before the arms were launched.

On a ResNet, “feeds a residual add” and “is the deepest, widest BatchNorm scale” are the same measurement seen twice — the campaign has never manipulated residual structure, which is why cvg1 runs an architecture with no residual additions at all. Separately, the best carrier-free triple that exists on this model still carries 225× less meta-gradient mass than the carriers, so cdep1 can rule out “any three matched layer4 BatchNorm parameters” but cannot separate tensor identity from term magnitude. No batch on this architecture can.

results/all_runs.csv

Every one of the 2,761 rows is ResNet — eleven network values, zero exceptions. That is the campaign’s single largest scope limit and the objection every tensor-level finding shares: “three layer4 BatchNorm scales” is a claim about one architecture’s parameter list until it is shown elsewhere.

It was never an infrastructure limit — and now it cannot be excused as one.

Scoping the port found the harness is architecture-agnostic: train.py touches the network through one build_network call, HF.py derives every granularity from named_parameters() and holds no architecture logic, and neither the args guard nor the launcher library carries a tensor-count assumption. A new architecture costs one if and one class. VGG11_bn_c100 is built, proved additive byte-for-byte, backward-compatible across all eleven existing network values, and launched. The caveat still stands on every finding until cvg1 lands — but it is now measurable rather than structural.
